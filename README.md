# RL and Optimization

This repo holds coursework and a final project for a Nonlinear Optimization course, based on Nocedal and Wright's *Numerical Optimization*, second edition. The homework sets work through chapters 2 through 6, covering unconstrained optimization, line search methods, conjugate gradient, and quasi Newton methods, then chapter 12, covering constrained optimization. The final project applies reinforcement learning to robot navigation and draws direct parallels between reinforcement learning and the numerical optimization methods studied earlier in the course.

## Coursework

### HW1, Chapter 2: Fundamentals of Unconstrained Optimization

Files: [HW1.m](HW1.m), [HW1.pdf](HW1.pdf)

Finds and classifies critical points of two objective functions, a stress formula for a plate with a hole (as a function of hole diameter) and the volume of an open box folded from a flat sheet. Both use symbolic differentiation in MATLAB, a sweep of initial guesses through fsolve to locate every critical point, and the sign of the second derivative to identify the minimizer among them. The stress problem is then solved a second way with plain gradient descent, and the script checks that the gradient descent result and the fsolve based result land on the same minimizer.

### HW2, Chapter 3: Line Search Methods

Files: [HW2.m](HW2.m), [HW2.pdf](HW2.pdf)

Applies Newton's method to find a root of g(x) = 7 sin(x) exp(minus x) minus 1, tracking the last few error terms to estimate the rate of convergence and confirm it is quadratic, and checking that different initial guesses can converge to different roots of the same function, compared against MATLAB's fzero. Also runs Newton's method on a two variable unconstrained optimization problem using a symbolic gradient and Hessian, and compares steepest descent against Newton's method on the Rosenbrock function, both with an Armijo backtracking line search, showing the zigzagging step behavior of steepest descent against the direct convergence of Newton's method.

### HW3, Chapters 5 and 6: Conjugate Gradient and Quasi Newton Methods

Files: [HW4.m](HW4.m), [HW4.pdf](HW4.pdf)

Note: this assignment is saved under the file name HW4 in the repo.

Runs linear conjugate gradient on Hilbert matrices of order 5, 8, 12, and 20 to show how the ill conditioning of the Hilbert matrix drives up the iteration count and the error against the exact solution as the dimension grows. Includes a proof that the search directions generated by conjugate gradient are pairwise conjugate. Implements the nonlinear Fletcher Reeves method on the Rosenbrock function and compares it against the steepest descent and Newton results from HW2, taking far fewer iterations than steepest descent but more than Newton, without ever forming a Hessian. Implements BFGS on the same problem, converging faster than Fletcher Reeves and competitive with Newton's method, while only ever using gradient information to build up an approximate Hessian. Also includes a bonus nonlinear least squares problem, fitting an exponential model to data with a Gauss Newton method built from a symbolic Jacobian.

### HW4, Chapters 3 and 4: Hessian Modification and Trust Region Methods

Files: [HW3.m](HW3.m), [HW3.pdf](HW3.pdf)

Note: this assignment is saved under the file name HW3.m and HW3.pdf in the repo.

Modifies an indefinite matrix by adding a multiple of the identity until its Cholesky factorization succeeds, restoring positive definiteness. Visualizes the contours of a quadratic model built from the Rosenbrock function's gradient and Hessian at a point, with trust region circles of different radii overlaid to show how the radius bounds the step. Implements the dogleg method inside a trust region framework on the Rosenbrock function, updating the trust region radius from the ratio of actual to predicted reduction and plotting the path of iterates over the Rosenbrock contours. Extends the same method to the n dimensional Rosenbrock function with 20 independent variables, and again with 60 for the bonus problem, both converging to the vector of ones.

### HW5, Chapter 12: Constrained Optimization

Files: [HW5.m](HW5.m), [HW5.pdf](HW5.pdf)

Verifies the first and second order KKT conditions for an equality constrained problem two ways, through direct analysis of the Lagrangian and through the null space method. Implements the active set method for an inequality constrained quadratic program, tracking which constraints become active across iterations as the algorithm moves toward the solution.

## Final Project: Application of Reinforcement Learning in Robot Navigation

Files: [Project.py](Project.py)

This project applies reinforcement learning to robot navigation, where an agent learns the shortest path from a starting position to a target through trial and error rather than being told the answer directly. It builds on a Reinforcement Learning Specialization completed on Coursera, based on the Sutton and Barto reinforcement learning textbook, and the accompanying report walks through the underlying theory, Markov decision processes, the Bellman equation, and policy evaluation and improvement, before implementing Q learning.

The method is tested on the Cliff Walking environment from OpenAI Gym (CliffWalking-v0), where an agent starts at one corner of a grid and must reach a goal at the opposite corner, with a strip of cells along the bottom representing a cliff. Stepping off the cliff costs a large penalty and resets the agent to the start, while every other move costs a small penalty, which pushes the agent to find the shortest safe path rather than wandering. The agent follows an epsilon greedy policy, exploring randomly some of the time and otherwise taking its best known action, updating a Q table with a learning rate of 0.5 and a discount rate of 1. Results are averaged over 100 independent runs of 200 episodes each, and the final learned policy is replayed in a rendered episode to show the path the agent settles on. The script also includes an exponential decay schedule for the exploration rate, which reduces exploration as the agent learns more and, per the report, sharply reduces the reward oscillations seen with a fixed exploration rate, pushing the agent closer to a fully deterministic optimal policy.

A significant part of the project draws direct parallels between reinforcement learning and the numerical optimization methods studied earlier in the course. Policy iteration in reinforcement learning mirrors iterative optimization methods like steepest descent and Newton's method, where a solution is progressively refined rather than solved in one step. The reward function plays a role similar to penalty and barrier methods in constrained optimization, shaping the objective by penalizing undesirable outcomes like falling off the cliff. The learning rate in Q learning plays a role similar to step size selection in backtracking line search, controlling how large an update is applied at each step.
